Margins of error. Each standard error is the sample standard deviation (n - 1) of the per-state figures
over the square root of the number of states at that distance (n/a with one state): for every score, and
for the model minus each program on the same states. Beside the count of states only the model gets, the
count expected if the model's successes were unrelated to the program's is m x p (m states the program
misses, p the share the model gets), with standard error sqrt(m p (1 - p)). In the same-wrong-turns table
each is across the cubes with a wrong try (n/a with fewer than two), because the tries on one cube are
not independent. These margins reflect the choice of test states only; for the surface readers they do
not say what a different training set would give. With 17 distances, an occasional gap of two standard
errors is expected by luck alone.
